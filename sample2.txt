The kerosene lamp in the corner of the room flickered weakly, turning the massive shadows on the wall into what seemed like ghosts. Raskolnikov leaned against the window sill, staring out into the pitch-black darkness. The storm inside him refused to subside; he was crushing under the weight of every step he took, under the perfect theory he had constructed in his mind. He wanted to believe he was right; he had repeated to himself dozens of times that everything was just a simple mathematical calculation made for the greater good of humanity, but the cold ache rising from the depths of his soul shattered this logic every single time.

"Are you still awake?" Razumihin asked, trying to hide the anxiety in his voice. The heavy air in the room was suffocating him too.

Raskolnikov did not turn around. "Sleep? For a man like me, sleep is nothing but a luxury now."

"You are torturing yourself," Razumihin said, rising from the table and taking a few steps toward his friend. "You haven’t eaten a thing for days, and your eyes are completely sunken. You walk the streets of St. Petersburg like a ghost. I don't know what you are chasing or what you're trying to prove, but the end of this road is dark, can't you see that?"

Raskolnikov turned around slowly. His face was as pale as a corpse's, yet there was an almost frantic glitter in his eyes.

"You don’t understand, Razumihin," he said, his voice no louder than a whisper, yet it seemed to echo off every object in the room. "You ordinary people are so accustomed to living within the safe boundaries the world has drawn for you that you cannot even fathom what it means to step beyond them. I am not trying to prove anything. I just... I just wanted to see if I had the strength to cross that line. And believe me, the other side of that line isn’t dark as you think; it is merely empty, consisting of nothing but a terrifying silence."

Razumihin stood frozen at what he had just heard. The abyss he saw in his friend's eyes for the very first time left him with absolutely nothing to say.
